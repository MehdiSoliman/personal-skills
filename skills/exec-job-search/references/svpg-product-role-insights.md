# SVPG — "A Fresh Definition of The Product Role" (Marty Cagan, Aug 2026)

Source: https://www.svpg.com/a-fresh-definition-of-the-product-role/ — key takeaways for VP positioning + interview prep.

## 1. The "Rorschach" interview question
Cagan has asked for decades: **"How do you define the product role?"** — the answer reveals the candidate's product model, why they think the role exists, what it contributes. No right/wrong answer; what you *emphasize* is the signal.

→ For Mehdi: his answer = his thesis (human-first + people→agents + "the hard part is knowing what should exist"), NOT generic "discover/deliver". His About already encodes this answer.

## 2. The 3 skills of an effective product person (via Benedict Evans, "Most People Aren't Tool Builders")
1. **Problem discovery** — seeing the GENERAL problem behind a specific pain/idea ("not everyone can spot these opportunities").
2. **Solution discovery (value risk)** — "People that are really good at using the tool are not the same people as those that are really good at creating the tool." / "Being good at sales does not make you good at making sales software."
3. **Viability (business fit)** — understanding the company deeply enough that the solution works for sales/marketing/finance/compliance/legal/legacy systems/workflows.

→ Mehdi's CV demonstrates all three, but **viability** is under-leveraged — it's exactly his "I think business" angle (investor, unicorn, profitability).

## 3. "Most people aren't tool builders" (the AI-era point)
- "AI makes it easy for anyone to build tools to automate their work… except most people and most companies aren't tool-builders, don't think like that, and can't and won't do that. This is why software companies and consultants exist — **AI changes the thresholds but not the problem**."
- "the hard part is knowing that it should exist, and knowing how it should exist, and that's a different person."

→ Validates the product role against "AI will replace PMs". Defensive argument for a VP in 2026. Nuances Mehdi's "people→agents" thesis: the durable skill isn't *who* the user is, it's *knowing what should exist and how*. Integrated into his About as: "But the hard part doesn't change: knowing what should exist, and how."
